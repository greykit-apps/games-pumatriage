## Design Context

### Users
Board-gamers who know and like **Dice Hospital**. They already hold the rules in their head; they come to PumaTriage for a faithful, fast digital table they can play solo or against AI rivals, at a desk, in the gaps of a day. Infosec practitioners are a secondary audience who get the jokes, but the game is judged first by someone comparing it to the cardboard.

### Brand Personality
Dry, practitioner, unhurried. greykit.com describes itself as "security-themed games and edutainment" and "a small arcade of bad ideas" from a 20-year security consultant who is "not a developer, allegedly". The voice is wry and brief, never zany, never emoji. The SIEM-feed log talks like a SIEM feed.

**Emotional goal: it should feel like Dice Hospital.** Brisk, tactile, satisfying dice-chucking worker placement with a little dwell-time pressure. Not grim, not a lecture on incident response. The SOC theme is a skin over the board game, not the point of it.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Cybersec-CRT tribe, non-negotiable:** phosphor green `#00ff41` on near-black, amber `#ffb000` for headings and the current thing, red `#ff3333` for danger; monospace type; scanline and vignette overlay. This is the shared identity of PumaSOC, PumaSecure, PumaPanic and PumaResponse. Design *within* it; do not fight the dark-mode-with-glow look, because here it is the house style rather than a default.
- **No real-hospital visuals.** No crosses, beds, scrubs or medical iconography. Wards are quarantine segments, patients are hosts, nurses are analysts.
- **Dice colours are just colours** (red / yellow / green), exactly as in the source game. Colour-blind accommodation is explicitly not a priority for this game, since the original is colour-only too.
- **Single file, offline, no dependencies** is a constraint the design must respect: no web fonts, no images, CSS-drawn dice.
- **Desktop only.** This is not a mobile game; the stacked phone layout merely has to not break. Judge and design at 1280–1600px.
- **Board order follows the physical board:** staff (one card per person, ability on the card) on top, tools in the middle, quarantine dice at the bottom, with the decision strip directly under the dice.

### Design Principles
1. **The table comes first.** Dice, quarantine segments and tools should read like a board-game table at a glance; chrome, panels and log recede behind them.
2. **A shift should take about as long as a round at the table.** Every phase that can resolve itself should; every confirm that can be a double-click should. Fewer clicks beats more explanation.
3. **Faithful before flavoured.** A Dice Hospital player must never have to re-learn a rule because of the theme. Themed names are a skin; rule text stays exact.
4. **Tribe identity is fixed, so spend design effort on hierarchy and rhythm**, not on palette or type. Amber marks the one thing to look at; green is the ground; red is only for harm.
5. **Practitioner voice.** Short, dry, specific. Log lines say what happened and to which host; help text explains once and gets out of the way.
